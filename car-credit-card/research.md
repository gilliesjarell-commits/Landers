# Research: Vehicle-Secured Credit Card (Yendo) · TikTok + Instagram + Facebook

## 1. Offer check
- **Slug:** `car-credit-card` (kept the brand out of the public URL; bridge pages don't name the brand).
- **Offer:** Yendo, a credit card secured by the applicant's vehicle instead of a cash deposit. Issued by
  Cross River Bank (NerdWallet). Yendo places a lien on the vehicle title and uses the car's value to help
  set the credit line; the cardholder keeps driving the car. If the account defaults, the vehicle can be
  repossessed and sold.
  - Published details (not used as claims on the pages): credit lines of about $450–$10,000; a 29.88%
    fixed purchase APR and $40 annual fee per Credit Karma / Mastercard listings (sources disagree on the
    APR); available only in some states; vehicle generally must be titled in the applicant's name.
- **Vertical:** Finance (credit card). Guardrails: no income or earnings claims; finance disclaimer on
  every page; no "guaranteed approval", "no credit check" or approval-odds claims; no numbers (APR, limits,
  fees) on pages or ads, which also avoids Truth in Lending "trigger term" disclosure requirements.
  The vehicle-as-collateral risk is stated plainly on every page.
- **EPC:** $1.20 (from the user; information only).
- **Affiliate link:** `https://afflat3e3.com/trk/lnk/5531C00A-712E-4ABF-A234-CC63ACF75F16/?o=27618&c=918277&a=794976&k=760ABAB9AE4F3A25D8EF8F4E24EFA674&l=29942`
  plus `&s1=<angle>` (CAKE sub-ID field s1, per the user).
- **Traffic:** TikTok, Instagram and Facebook (overrides the default).
- **Test budget:** $50 split across the 3 angles (~$16.67 each).

### Traffic source policy check: allowed, with restrictions
- **Meta (Facebook, Instagram): Special Ad Category required.** Credit cards fall under **Financial
  Products and Services**, required for US advertisers or US audiences. It removes age, gender and ZIP
  targeting and narrows detailed targeting, so lean on broad targeting and creative. Ads also must not
  assert personal attributes ("Bad credit?", "Were you denied?"); the ad copy uses second-person
  scenarios instead.
- **TikTok: allowed with restrictions.** TikTok's Financial Services policy lists credit cards as
  allowed for licensed issuers, with required disclosures and an 18+ audience; some financial categories
  need pre-authorization through a TikTok sales rep. TikTok updated this policy in June 2026, so confirm
  the current rules (and whether affiliates of a card issuer can run these ads) in the TikTok Ads Help
  Center before spending.
- Both: no misleading claims about approval, rates or credit repair; landing pages carry the finance
  disclaimer, the collateral risk, the affiliate disclosure, and Privacy / Terms links.

## 2. Avatar
1. **Who:** US adults roughly 25–55, lower-to-middle income, working (often hourly or gig), who own a car
   that's paid off or mostly paid off. Thin or damaged credit after medical bills, job loss or a divorce.
   They depend on the car for work. Daily frustration: one surprise expense away from a crisis.
2. **Top 10 pain points:**
   1. Denied for regular credit cards.
   2. Secured cards want a cash deposit they don't have.
   3. A surprise car repair or bill with no cushion.
   4. Payday and title loans with steep costs.
   5. Feeling judged by a score that reflects old problems.
   6. Wanting to rebuild credit but not knowing how to start.
   7. Paying more for everything because of low credit.
   8. Juggling bills and late fees.
   9. Embarrassment asking family for money.
   10. Fear of scams and predatory lenders targeting people like them.
3. **Already tried:** applying for cards and being denied, prepaid debit cards, secured cards with
   deposits, payday advances, cash-advance apps, title loans, borrowing from family, credit-builder loans.
4. **What makes them click now:** an urgent expense (car repair, rent gap), a recent denial, or a clear,
   respectful explanation of an option they hadn't heard of: "your car could help you get a card".
5. **Where they are online:** Facebook (local groups, Marketplace), TikTok personal-finance and
   "credit tips" creators, Instagram reels, YouTube, Reddit (r/CRedit, r/personalfinance).

**Audience notes:** Reddit and review sites describe Yendo as a legitimate option for thin or damaged
credit; common complaints are lines lower than expected, lien and title delays, and app issues
(Trustpilot ratings range from about 3.2 to 3.9 depending on the snapshot). The pages set expectations
honestly: approval and the credit line depend on the person and the vehicle.

## 3. Angle scores
| Angle | Score | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pain Point | 5 | The denial, the deposit you don't have, the surprise repair: concrete moments this person knows well. |
| Curiosity | 4 | "Could your car help you get a credit card?" is a real surprise for most people, and the click closes the loop. |
| Social Proof | 2 | Reviews are mixed and conflicting, and the offer owner supplied no numbers. Guardrail: skip. |
| Fear | 1 | The audience is financially vulnerable, and fear appeals in credit feel predatory and draw ad rejections. Guardrail: skip. |
| Direct Benefit | 4 | "Use your car's value, with no cash deposit, and keep driving it" is a clear, concrete result. |

**Picked:** Pain Point, Curiosity, Direct Benefit. **Skipped:** Social Proof (no real proof), Fear
(vulnerable audience, guardrail).

## Formats
Finance plus cold social traffic points to Quiz; the Pain story reads best as an article. Two formats.
- **Pain → Advertorial (`pain.html`):** the denial-and-deposit story, plus an honest explanation of the
  trade-off, needs room an article gives.
- **Curiosity → Quiz (`curiosity.html`):** "Could your car help you get a credit card?" is a natural
  quiz opener; vehicle questions are easy taps for cold TikTok traffic.
- **Direct Benefit → Quiz (`benefit.html`):** stays in the Quiz format to keep the test at 2 formats;
  "what would a credit line help with" turns the benefit into picture answers.
ZIP code is the last quiz question because availability depends on the state.

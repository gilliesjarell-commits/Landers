# Research: Solar Generator (TikTok + Instagram + Facebook)

## 1. Offer check
- **Slug:** `solar-generator`
- **Vertical:** Other (consumer product: portable solar generator / power station for home backup and
  off-grid use). Not insurance, finance or health, so no regulated-vertical line. Every page still
  carries a product disclaimer: runtime varies, and it is not a whole-home backup system.
- **Offer:** solar generator, through the affiliate link below. The offer page wasn't opened (opening the
  tracking link would record a click), so the pages don't describe the product's brand, size, price or
  specs. They presell the category and send people to the offer page for the details.
- **EPC:** $1.69 (from the user; information only).
- **Affiliate link:** `https://afflat3e1.com/trk/lnk/5531C00A-712E-4ABF-A234-CC63ACF75F16/?o=31558&c=918277&a=794976&k=B35EE24D4BCADACAA2946DD48B0DD63A&l=37137`
  plus `&sub=<angle>` on each page.
- **Traffic:** TikTok, Instagram and Facebook (overrides the Facebook + Native default).
- **Test budget:** $50 split across the 3 angles (~$16.67 each), one ad set per angle.

### Traffic source policy check: allowed
- **Meta (Facebook, Instagram):** consumer electronics and home backup power aren't in Meta's prohibited
  or restricted categories, and no Special Ad Category applies. Watch the usual rules: no claims the
  landing page doesn't back up, no fake urgency, no personal-attribute wording ("Your power keeps going
  out"), no fake UI in images.
- **TikTok:** not a prohibited or restricted category in TikTok's ad policies. TikTok expects truthful,
  substantiated product claims, so the copy avoids runtime numbers, savings claims and "never lose power"
  promises. The pages are mobile-first and link the Privacy Policy in every footer, as TikTok requires.
- Nothing needs certification. The LEGAL block carries the Meta and TikTok non-affiliation lines.

## 2. Avatar
On TikTok, Instagram and Facebook this person isn't searching for a generator. They're scrolling, and
the ad has to catch the memory of the last time the lights went out.

1. **Who:** US homeowners and renters, roughly 30–65, middle income, suburban or rural, often in storm,
   hurricane, wildfire or ice-storm regions. Families with kids, remote workers who lose Wi-Fi, people
   with older relatives at home, plus campers, RV owners and weekend cabin people. Practical, not
   "prepper", but they hate feeling helpless when the power goes out.
2. **Top 10 pain points:**
   1. Food in the fridge and freezer spoiling during a long outage.
   2. Phones dying, with no way to call family or check the outage map.
   3. Losing Wi-Fi and being unable to work from home.
   4. Sitting in the dark with candles and flashlights, kids bored and anxious.
   5. Gas generators: loud all night, annoy the neighbors, hard to start.
   6. Storing gas, stale fuel, and lines at gas stations during storms.
   7. Exhaust: a gas generator has to run outside, away from windows and doors.
   8. Not knowing what size or type of backup they actually need.
   9. Whole-home standby generators feel too expensive and need installation.
   10. Feeling unprepared every storm season and promising to "deal with it later".
3. **Already tried:** candles and flashlights, phone power banks, running the car to charge phones, an old
   gas generator in the garage, borrowing a neighbor's extension cord, a cooler with ice, or nothing.
4. **What makes them click now:** a recent outage or a storm in the forecast, seeing a neighbor's loud
   generator, camping and RV season, or a short video showing a quiet unit keeping a fridge and lamp on.
   The ad has to show a moment they recognize and offer a calm, simple next step ("see the solar option").
5. **Where they are online:** TikTok (outage, prepping, camping, van-life and home-hack videos), Facebook
   (neighborhood and town groups, Marketplace, homesteading and RV groups), Instagram reels, YouTube
   reviews, Reddit (r/preppers, r/SolarDIY, r/GoRVing, r/homeowners).

**Competitor and audience notes:** power-station brands run "gas vs. solar" comparisons and lead with
quiet, no fumes and indoor use; UGC-style "my power went out, here's what I did" videos are common on
TikTok. Comparison articles point out the real limits: battery capacity sets runtime, and heavy loads
(central air, well pumps) still favor gas. The pages state those limits plainly, which builds trust.

**Category facts (for planning; the pages use only the general, safety-agency point):**
- Gas generators must run outdoors, away from windows and doors, because their exhaust contains carbon
  monoxide (CPSC guidance, summarized by PIRG). The pages say this without numbers.
- US customers averaged roughly 11 hours of electricity interruptions in 2024, most of it from major
  events (EIA data via EC&M). Not verified on eia.gov from this session, so it isn't used on the pages.

## 3. Angle scores (for a TikTok / Meta scroller)
| Angle | Score | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pain Point | 5 | The dark kitchen, the dying phone, the fridge on a timer: nearly everyone has a recent outage story, and it's easy to show in a feed. |
| Curiosity | 4 | "Still counting on a gas generator?" opens a real question: what's the quiet alternative? The click closes the loop. |
| Social Proof | 2 | No reviews or numbers from the offer owner, and no verifiable public proof to point to. Guardrail: skip. |
| Fear | 3 | Outages and generator exhaust are real, but fear on TikTok and Meta draws closer review, and Pain already covers the moment without scare tactics. |
| Direct Benefit | 4 | "Keep the fridge, lights and Wi-Fi on" is a clear, concrete result people picture instantly. |

**Picked:** Pain Point, Curiosity, Direct Benefit. **Skipped:** Social Proof (no real proof, guardrail),
Fear (lower score and more ad-review risk than the other three).

## Formats
Cold social traffic, a home-backup niche, and TikTok (quiz-friendly) point to Quiz, with one story page
for the Pain angle. Two formats in the test: Advertorial and Quiz.
- **Pain → Advertorial (`pain.html`):** a pain story reads best as an article, and Facebook / Instagram
  users are used to tapping into articles from the feed.
- **Curiosity → Quiz (`curiosity.html`):** "Still counting on a gas generator?" is a natural quiz opener,
  and the low effort suits cold TikTok traffic.
- **Direct Benefit → Quiz (`benefit.html`):** "What would you keep running?" turns the benefit into
  picture answers (fridge, Wi-Fi, lights, laptop); it stays in the Quiz format to keep the test at 2 formats.

Every house runs on a handful of machines most of us never think about: the air conditioner, the furnace, the water heater, the kitchen appliances. They work quietly for years. Then one morning one of them doesn't.

When that happens, the problem isn't only the broken part. It's everything that comes with it: finding someone who can come out, wondering if the quote is fair, and working out how to pay for a bill you didn't plan for. Most homeowners face that moment sooner or later, and most aren't set up for it.

You've probably lived this

It's the first truly hot week of the year. You wake up and the house feels heavy. You check the thermostat. The AC is running, but the air coming out is warm.

You start calling. The first company can't come until Thursday. The second one can come today, for an after-hours fee. You take the afternoon off work, and you wait.

The tech finally shows up, looks at the unit, and gives you a number. You don't know if it's fair. You just know you need the house cool again, so you say yes.

None of that is a failure on your part. Systems wear out on their own schedule, and homeowners insurance generally doesn't pay for things that simply wear out. That gap is where the stress lives.

The breakdown is the part you can't control. The scramble after it is the part you can.

What a home warranty does

A home warranty is a service contract. You pay for a plan, and when a covered system or appliance breaks down from normal wear and tear, you make one call. The company sends a technician, and you pay a set service fee for the visit. Covered repairs, or a replacement when repair isn't possible, are handled under the plan's terms and limits.

When something breaks today

With a home warranty plan

What a plan can include

  • Major systems: heating, cooling, plumbing and electrical.
  • Kitchen and laundry appliances.
  • Or both, depending on the plan you pick.
  • Repair, or replacement when a covered item can't be repaired, within the plan's limits.

Read this before you pick a plan

Home warranties aren't all the same, and the fine print matters. Before you buy any plan, check the service fee, what's excluded, any payout limits per item, the waiting period before coverage starts, and what maintenance the plan expects from you. A good plan is one you understand before you need it.

Common questions

Isn't this what homeowners insurance is for?

Homeowners insurance is built for sudden damage like fire or storms. It generally doesn't pay when a system or appliance simply wears out. A home warranty is a separate service contract for those breakdowns.

Does a plan cover everything?

No. Every plan has exclusions, payout limits and a waiting period. Read the contract so you know what's covered before you need it.
